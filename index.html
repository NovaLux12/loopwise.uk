What you get · each morning · each week · always

Three things, at three times of day.

No dashboard to learn and no subscription to remember. Three things arrive on their own, and you stay in charge of every decision.

01 01 · Each morning

A short read of your night

A paragraph in plain English, pointing at the parts of the overnight trace worth a second look. No decisions are made for you. It flags, you decide.

02 02 · Each week

A one-page report for your clinic

One page you can email to your diabetes team: averages, time in range, lows, highs, and a breakdown for each day. Every number can be traced back to the reading it came from.

03 03 · Always

A record of everything

Anything the tools ever change gets written to a log file on your own computer. You can open it, read it, or hand it to your clinic. Nothing happens without a record.

How it works · three steps

Your pump already uploads. This just reads it back to you.

You do not change anything about how your pump works, and you do not have to enter readings by hand. Three things sit between your sensor and a paragraph of plain English.

Honest caveat

It is not zero-setup

Getting started means running a small piece of software yourself, or having someone you trust run it for you. If that sounds like a barrier, it is a fair reason to wait. The pilot programme exists to help with exactly this.

Your call

Nothing leaves your machine by default

The data copy runs on your own hardware, pointed at your own Nightscout. The morning brief and weekly report are the only parts the maintainer runs for you, and you can switch either one off and keep the rest.

No vendor

No account, no subscription, no data broker

There is no login and nothing to sign up for. The open-source parts are yours to keep: if the maintainer stopped tomorrow, the bridge and the reporting tools would still be running on your own machine, doing exactly the same thing.

Is this for you?

It might suit you. It might not. Here is the honest version.

This is research and educational tooling, built by one person, in the early days. If any of this does not fit your situation, the answer is simply no.

Might suit you

This could be for you

If these are true
  • You have Type 1 diabetes · as an adult, or as a parent or carer of a child who does.
  • You use a Medtronic pump and sensor · the 780G with a Guardian 4 or Simplera sensor is what is being tested against.
  • You want a short read, not another dashboard. If you already have three apps open and none of them tell you anything, this is aimed at you.
  • You are comfortable with a little setup, or you know someone who is, or you would like help with it.
Might not suit you

This is not for you if

Please do not force it
  • You need it to be a medical device. It is not one. It is not FDA approved, and it is not clinical software.
  • You want it to dose insulin. It will never do that, and nothing here should ever be used to make a dosing decision.
  • You rely on official alarms. It does not replace or add to the safety notifications Medtronic sends you.
  • You want support on your terms. There is one maintainer, in the UK evenings, replying in one to two working days.

Questions people actually ask

Questions we get asked.

If yours is not here, email the maintainer. The inbox is read by a person. There are also more technical answers, including what all the acronyms mean.

What exactly is Loopwise Health?

A small project run by one person. It copies your pump and sensor data out of Medtronic's CareLink service into Nightscout, an open-source diabetes platform you run yourself, then turns that data into a short morning brief and a one-page report for your clinic.

Is it safe? Does it dose insulin for me?

No. Nothing here doses insulin or replaces official CareLink safety notifications. It is research and educational tooling only (not a medical device, not FDA approved), and every treatment decision stays with you and your diabetes team; see the security model and scope.

Which pumps and CGMs does the bridge support?

Medtronic 780G pumps with a Guardian 4 or Simplera sensor is what is actively being tested, because that is the maintainer's own setup. Other Medtronic pumps that upload to CareLink will probably work but have not been properly tested, so treat them as untested rather than supported.

How much does it cost?

Free. All four tools are MIT-licensed, there is no paid version, no subscription, and nothing is tracked. The only cost is whatever you choose to spend on a small computer or server to run it on, and you can run it on hardware you already own.

Can my clinic use the reports?

That is the point of the whole thing. The reports already produce a machine-readable file plus a breakdown for each day, so any number can be checked against the readings it came from. The polished, clinic-ready version is what is being built now. Ask for it and you will be told honestly whether it is ready yet.

Where does my data live?

On a Nightscout site that you run yourself. The bridge posts there and nowhere else, and nothing is sent back to us. No tracking, no telemetry, no account. Anything the tools ever change gets recorded in a log file on your own computer, which you can open and read.

Are you affiliated with Medtronic?

No. Loopwise is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Medtronic. It builds on open-source work by other people in the Nightscout community, and if the original project wakes up again, the fixes get handed back.

What is a CareLink-to-Nightscout bridge?

CareLink is Medtronic's cloud service: your pump and sensor upload readings to it. Nightscout is an open-source platform you host yourself for viewing those readings. The bridge signs into CareLink the way the official app does and copies new pump and CGM data into your Nightscout every five minutes. See the bridge.

What is Nightscout?

A free, open-source remote monitor for glucose data, maintained by the #WeAreNotWaiting community. You host your own instance; it stores CGM readings, treatments and device status. Every Loopwise tool reads from your Nightscout, or writes to it, and nowhere else.
